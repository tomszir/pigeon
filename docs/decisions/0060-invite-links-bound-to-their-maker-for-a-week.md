# 0060: Invite links bound to their maker, for a week

An invite link names the device that made it and when it ends, at most a week after it's made; whoever opens it takes cards and answers of every kind only from that device, and asks through it only before its end; nothing is closed when a device is removed; and links from before (`a/`) no longer open.

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27

## Context

Every device on your list holds the secret of each invite link it heard of, and one taken off the list keeps it ([0051](0051-replacing-secrets-after-a-removal.md)), with the link's claim key ([0053](0053-claimed-invite-cards.md)). Until now, what stopped a removed device from adding people as you through those links was closing: each device still on the list that heard of the removal put a closed card, signed on the newer list, for every link open then, its own and the others', and answered requests through a removed device's links `closed` until two days after each ended.

That didn't hold. A link's end lived only in its card, and the card is written by whichever device puts it. Once the closed cards lapsed with the link's end, the removed device could put a fresh open card of its own on the list from before, saying the link lasts weeks longer; whoever opened the link then saw your real name and fingerprint, asked through that card, and was added by the removed device as you, with nothing ever correcting them.

A first fix, drafted under the number 0057 and never released, put the end in a new link format, `#b/`, retired `a/` links 30 days after the release, and kept the closing, with more rules around it: the asker read the link's cards again before taking `added`, an open card had to name every device on its log, requests went to the official Lighthouses too, closed cards went to every official Lighthouse and the one a link names, outlived the link by the asker's two days, were put again before they lapsed, and covered links that had ended in the two days before a removal. A model of removal and invite links, explored over every short history, and rounds of review against the code each found a new way around that closing (a card naming only the removed device winning a tie, a link's own Lighthouse nobody closed it at, closed cards lapsing inside the asker's window, a removal after a link's end, closed cards only at the closer's own Lighthouses). The last one found was the worst: a device that turns hostile while still on the list tells your other devices a false list of its links (leaving one out, giving it an earlier end, saying it's closed, or calling another device's link its own), so they close and watch too briefly or not at all, and on its own links whoever opens one is fooled for good, with no fault and every device on mail. A link it makes and never tells of is never closed at all, so no rule about the list can protect its own links below their end.

The same model then compared four designs with the removed device hostile from the start: binding links to their maker on top of the closing; closing with rules that distrust what the removed device says; live codes only; and binding links to their maker with the end in the link and no closing at all. The last was the simplest and left the least: on links other devices made, nothing, flooding included; on the removed device's own links, use until their end. Each of its rules was tried off, and the explorations show which ones carry it.

A first review of this design found that the binding covered only `added`: a removed device that had read a request through another device's link, sealed to it while it was still on the list, could still answer it `declined`, `busy` or `closed`, and the request ended there; if the maker's `added` came after it in the same fetch, the maker counted the person as a friend while they counted the maker as having said no. The model had let the removed device answer only `added`. It now answers with every kind, and the rule below, every answer from the maker alone, leaves it nothing.

A second review found the exception that first allowed one answer from another device, a `closed` whose log takes the maker off the list, forgeable: the first device, which writes epoch 0, stolen and left off by a recovery that keeps the maker, can sign the maker's removal onto its own copy of epoch 0, and an asker that opened the link before the recovery, and sealed its request to the stolen device too, takes it, so the maker's `added` after it is dropped. The model of recovery epochs reproduces it in 9 steps, and finds nothing without the exception. So there is none.

The constraints: no new Lighthouse request or record; nothing that rests on what a removed device says, or on anyone being on mail; links already given out can't change; anything compared has one encoding.

## Decision

- **A link names its maker and its end.** It is `#b/<secret><fingerprint><maker><end>`: the secret and the identity's long fingerprint as before, the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of the device that made it (26 base32 characters), and the time it ends in whole seconds since 1970, five bytes big-endian (8 base32 characters, with no bits left over). 86 characters, all lowercase: that is the one spelling read, so a link is never read differently from how Pigeon wrote it, and the maker and fingerprint it names are compared as bytes. Then `?l=` when it names a Lighthouse. The card's `expires` is that same time.
- **Only the maker counts.** Whoever opens a link takes a card only if it is signed by the device the link names, active on the log the card carries, open or closed. An asker takes every answer, `added`, `declined`, `busy` or `closed`, only from that device, kept with the request when it's sent; another device's answer is dropped and the request goes on waiting. There is no exception, not even a `closed` whose log takes the maker off the list: a device a recovery took off can sign such a log on the epoch before the recovery, and an asker that hasn't heard of the recovery can't tell it from a real removal. What that costs is what an older Pigeon on your list sends for a removed device's own link, which now goes unread: that link goes silent, as below. The maker is the only device that puts cards or answers through a link anyway, so this costs an honest Pigeon nothing, and every other device of yours, removed or not, can do nothing through a link it didn't make: not add anyone, and not end someone's request with a no.
- **A new reply secret after an answer.** Asking again through a link keeps the reply secret only while the request before still waits, so an answer already on its way still arrives; after an answer it draws a new one, so a device that read the first request can't answer the next under its tags.
- **The end is the link's.** An open card whose `expires` is later than the link's end is refused. Nobody opens or asks through a link on or after its end, whatever card they were shown before. A link may last 1 hour, 1 day or 7 days; one that says it ends more than 7 days and a day from now is refused, the day being for clocks that disagree, so a link made to end later opens only in its last week and a day.
- **Nothing is closed on a removal.** No device closes another's links, puts closed cards for them, answers requests through them, or watches their ask tags, and a removal doesn't close a device's own links either. The list of links your devices tell each other stays only so Invite Links can say how many are open elsewhere; nothing about a removal reads it, and the claim key is no longer in it.
- **`a/` links stop at this release.** They name no maker and no end, so nothing could tell a removed device's answer through one from the maker's. Opening one says it was made by an older Pigeon and to ask for a new one. Your own are closed when Pigeon starts, which tells whoever is waiting on one, and leave Your Links; an add Pigeon closed in the middle of on one is left unfinished rather than finished, so it isn't sent. That doesn't cover an add an older Pigeon had already gone through on your side, befriending them, when it closed just before sending the answer: after the update that one stays added on your side alone. A request sent through one before the update stops waiting and says the link was made by an older Pigeon. There is no transition: an `a/` link kept open for 30 more days would be open to a removed device, including another device's, unless the closing were kept for those days, with every gap above.
- **0057 is replaced, not amended.** It never reached a release: almost every rule in it is deleted here, and a record accepted and superseded in the same change would describe a design no Pigeon ever ran. What it kept, the end in a `#b/` link and the end check, is stated here, and its number is left unused.
- **This supersedes** the parts of earlier decisions that say otherwise: in [0038](0038-invite-links-through-lighthouses.md), the `a/` link, the 30-day lifetime, and your other devices closing a removed device's invites; in [0051](0051-replacing-secrets-after-a-removal.md), closing every link open at a removal; in [0053](0053-claimed-invite-cards.md), sharing the claim key with your other devices for their closed cards, and answering requests through a removed device's links `closed`.

## Consequences

A removed device can do nothing through a link another device made: its cards aren't chosen and none of its answers is taken, before its removal and after, whatever it told your other devices. It can't stretch any link past the end the link carries. Nothing it says about its links is read by anything that matters to a removal, and nothing depends on a closed card being up, read, put again or kept, or on any device being on mail. Lighthouses carry less: no closed cards on a removal, no re-puts, no second read before `added`.

What people pay, and what is left:

- **Links last a week.** The 30-day choice goes. A link kept in a profile or a signature has to be made again and shared each week.
- **A removed device's own links work for it until they end.** Shared or not, at most 7 days after each was made, plus the two days an asker who asked before the end goes on taking answers. Whoever it adds takes it as you until they hear your newer list some other way. That is the residual this accepts.
- **A removed device's links go silent instead of showing closed.** Its card stays up until the link's end; an honest removed device stops talking, so whoever asks gets no answer and gives up two days after the end.
- **Other devices' links survive a removal.** Removing one device no longer stops the links your other devices shared, which is what people expect; and to stop a link, you Delete it.
- **Older Pigeons** can't open a `#b/` link until they reload. An older device on your list makes `a/` links, which updated Pigeons refuse; acting on a removal, it still puts closed cards, which nobody takes now (they aren't the maker's), and answers `closed` for a removed device's links, which an asker drops now, as it isn't from the link's maker: that link goes silent.
- **Every `a/` link stops opening at this release**, at most 30 days of links, and needs sharing again; a request through one still waiting gets no `added`.
- **Lost or replaced devices.** A lost device's links get no answers, as before. Recovery keeps the ids of the devices it keeps, so their links work; a device linked again under a new id can't serve its old links, as before.
- A link is 86 characters after `#b/`, up from 52, and opening one hashes each card's signer once.

Ruled out:

- **Binding to the maker on top of closing**: it gains nothing on other devices' links over this, and keeps the false-list attack on the removed device's own.
- **Closing with rules that distrust the removed device**: stronger on its shared links, closed at the removal rather than open until their end, but every link heard is kept closed for 33 days, every removal puts a closed card for each, the limits of closing (a tab closed mid-removal, flooding before the end) stay on every device's links, and the removed device's unshared links are still open to their end.
- **Live codes only**: no residual worth the name, but no offline invites, the reason links exist.
- **A 30-day transition for `a/` links**: see above.
- **Sealing requests to the maker only, in place of checking who sent each answer**: the same protection, but a request already sealed to every device on an older card would still reach the removed device; one check on the answer covers both.
