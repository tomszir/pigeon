# Protocol

How peers identify themselves, find each other, and exchange messages, letters and pages. Identity, envelopes, chat logs, pair sessions and pairing are implemented in `@pigeon/core` and run in the browser over Trystero. Sealing mail, the Lighthouse that holds it ([lighthouse.md](lighthouse.md)) and letters are built; files and pages are specified here but not built yet.

## Identity

- A peer is a WebCrypto signing keypair (Ed25519, ECDSA P-256 where Ed25519 is missing) plus an ECDH P-256 keypair for sealing. `PeerId` is the base32 public signing key.
- The keypair is made on the first visit and kept in IndexedDB; the private key is non-extractable, so not even the page can read it. Clearing site data is a new identity.
- A `PeerId` is 52 base32 characters for an Ed25519 key (32 bytes) or 104 for a raw P-256 point (65 bytes); the length says which. Core never holds keys: it signs through a `Signer` and verifies and hashes through `Crypto`, which the platform implements.
- Handles are self-chosen, not unique, at most 32 characters; petnames are what you call a contact. Pairing shows a 16-hex-character fingerprint (the first 8 bytes of the id's SHA-256, spelled in fours) to compare.
- Everyone's picture is a pixel pigeon drawn from their id ([design.md](design.md#avatars)). `hello` says which version of the drawing to use, so a newer version never changes the bird of someone who already has one.
- Each device gets a sealing (ECDH P-256) keypair, announced in `hello` and bound to the device by its own signature, so mail, invites and letters can be sealed to devices. `@pigeon/core` seals and opens mail; announcing the key in `hello` isn't built yet.

## Devices

One person can use Pigeon on several devices. Each device has its own non-extractable key, and an identity is a device log (`devices/device-log.ts`). The first device's key starts the log and stays the identity's `PeerId`, so friends, fingerprints and avatars never change; it's on the list without an entry.

One device writes the log at a time: the first device, until a recovery key names another (below). Entries `{ seq, prev, kind, device, name?, at, sig }` are signed by the writer over the entry and the identity, each naming the signature of the one before: `add` puts a device on, named; `remove` takes one off for good within its epoch (its key can't be added again, and the writer can't be removed); `name` renames one. With one writer each epoch's entries are a single chain, and a copy further along it replaces a shorter one; a copy that disagrees on an entry both have is ignored. A log holds at most 100 entries across all its epochs. When a merge would hold more, the oldest epochs lose their last entries first, and the current epoch is never cut. No other device can change the list, so a stolen second device can do nothing to it.

A recovery key outranks every device. It's an Ed25519 key whose public half the first device commits to once, as `recovery: { key, at, sig }`, signed over `{ identity, type: "recovery-key", key, at }`. Where two copies of a log name different keys, the one you had first stays. A recovery is an item of `epochs`: `{ recover, entries }`, where `recover` is `{ epoch, writer, keep: [{ device, name }], next, at, sig }`, signed by the key current before it (`recovery.key` for epoch 1, the previous `next` after) over `{ identity, type: "recover", ... }`. Its `keep` (up to 8 devices, the writer among them) is the list from then on, and every other device is removed as of its `at`. The writer signs that epoch's entries, whose signed bytes add `epoch`. Each epoch stands on its own: its recover's signature and its own chain decide whether it holds, and the list comes from the latest epoch alone. An older epoch's entries only say who was once yours and until when, so a stolen device that writes more of them after a recovery changes nothing and can't make a log fail. Where two copies disagree on an epoch's recover, the one you hold stays if you hold a later epoch on top of it, so a used and replaced key can't start its epoch again. At your latest epoch the lower signature wins, with everything after it. Copies that agree take the one with more epochs, and within an epoch the longer copy of the same chain wins. Committing a key also signs a `name` entry for the first device, so a Pigeon from before, which takes a log only when it has more entries and then keeps it whole, keeps the commitment too. Epoch 0's entries and their bytes are unchanged, so Pigeons from before read them and ignore `recovery` and `epochs`. A log holds up to 8 epochs.

A recovered device's log is a tail: `from` is its first epoch, `by` the key that signed its recover, and it has nothing before that. Only a Pigeon that already has your earlier epochs takes a tail, by checking it follows on from them, and the tail's own device takes a whole log only if that log's key for its first epoch is `by`. Pairing checks whoever answers against the log you already keep for them, loaded from storage if need be. That turns away a tail from anyone you don't know, an old copy that would undo a recovery you've seen, and a device (a stolen first device, say) that speaks for itself when your copy of its log says a recovery took it off the list. A friend who keeps a log with epochs sends it back to its owner in their pair room, so a recovered device gets its whole log from the first friend it pairs with again. That friend's Pigeon knows them already, so pairing again keeps the chat and petname, takes the new pair secret and moves to its rooms.

Envelopes and chat entries are signed by a device. In a pair room a friend takes envelopes from any device active on your log, as theirs is to you, and chat log authors are devices, so sequence numbers never collide; presence shows the newest sent. A removed device's entries from before its removal (by their time, which is the device's own word) still count, so a device linked later catches up with no gaps; nothing later is taken from it. A device that finds itself removed stops talking. Your own other devices in the same pair room catch the chat up with each other (`log-sync`, `log-entries`) but aren't the friend. An identity with a log sends it as `device-log` before `hello`, in every pair room and again whenever it changes; friends keep it under `devices:<id>`. `hello` carries `devices: true`, and a friend whose `hello` lacks it is on a Pigeon from before device logs: it hears only your first device, and a second device's chat says so until they update.

A copied browser profile holds the same device key as the original. Every `hello` carries `instance`, 10 random bytes in base32 made each time the Messenger starts, and a device that hears a hello signed by its own key with another `instance` knows it's open in another browser too. Only one tab per browser runs the Messenger, so a second tab is never mistaken for a copy.

Linking is in person, from the first device. It hosts `link:<code>` (a code like an invite's, for 10 minutes) and shows it as the link `#l/<code><fingerprint>` in a QR code and in letters. The new device, which must have no friends or devices of its own (a Pigeon that has them can leave first, telling its friends and forgetting everything), joins and sends `link-request`; the existing one answers `link-offer` with whose it is (and its device log, if it isn't the first device), and both show the same 6-digit check, from SHA-256 of `pigeon/link/v1`, the code and both device keys. Confirming on the first device signs `add` and sends `link-accept` with a bundle (refused beforehand, with nothing signed, when it wouldn't fit one envelope): the identity, the device log, the self secret, the profile, the friends (with their pair secrets), the blocked list and friends' device logs. Chats aren't in it; they catch up through pair rooms. The new device keeps it all and starts again as that identity. The profile is copied then; afterwards your device log and your name and personal message are kept in step through the self room and the self mailbox, and your Lighthouse list through the self mailbox; the rest of your own data isn't kept in step between devices.

Your devices meet in `self:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(self secret, "pigeon/self/v1/" + UTC date)>`, locked with HKDF of the self secret (info `pigeon/self/password`) and renamed daily like pair rooms. Device logs travel there, so a removal reaches your other devices with no friend online, and so does `profile`, your name and personal message with when they last changed; a device takes one newer than its own. A device that finds itself removed stops talking and says so.

The self mailbox carries the same to a device that's offline, and your Lighthouse list too: a `mail:self` letter, sealed to each of your other devices still on your list, under the self secret's tags, at every Lighthouse on this device's list and on the list your other devices last said in the self room. Its body is a snapshot, `{ log, keys, profile?, lighthouses?, invites? }`: your device log, the sealing keys this device knows for your devices (its own included), and your name and your Lighthouse list (`{ list, changedAt }`), each with when it last changed, and the invite links this device made (see Invite links). It's sent when a change is made on this device (the list, a device's name, the recovery key, a link, your name or your Lighthouses) and when a device's sealing key is heard for the first time, so a device linked while another was away can be written to. A new one takes the place of an older one still waiting on the same devices, so a device that's away has one snapshot waiting, not one per change. A device fetching mail opens everything at all its Lighthouses before acting on any of it, and merges the device log of every `mail:self` letter first, whoever it came from, since a log is signed by whoever wrote it; the rest of a letter counts only from a device on the list that results, so a removal counts whichever letter was listed first. Each key counts only when signed by its own device and that device is on the list; the name and the list are taken when newer than this device's own and no more than a day ahead of its clock. A Lighthouse list kept from before devices shared theirs counts as changed when the Pigeon first starts with this, and is sent then. A device a removal takes off the list, or a recovery leaves off, is sent a letter of its own holding only the device log, so it hears and stops talking even if it never meets another of your devices.

## Your data in a file

Export My Data writes a `.txt` whose sealed line is `pigeon-sealed:v1:<rounds>:<salt>:<iv>:<sealed>` (base64), made with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 rounds) and AES-256-GCM from a passphrase. Inside it is the canonical JSON `{ kind: "pigeon-export", v: 1, at, self, profile, friends, blocked, friendDevices, logs, settings }`, where `logs` maps each friend to their chat log as stored. It holds no keys, device log or self secret. Import refuses a file whose `self` isn't this device's identity, merges each chat's entries with the same checks a catch-up uses, adds friends and blocks this device lacks (keeping its own where both have one), takes the profile and settings only on a device with no name yet, and starts the Messenger again.

## Rooms

All traffic goes through the `Transport` interface.

Inside a room the transport names connections (`ConnectionId`); those are not identities. A connection becomes a friend only by sending an envelope signed by that friend's key, starting with `hello`.

- `pair:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/pair/v2/" + UTC date)>`: where two friends meet, named from the 256-bit pair secret only they hold, so it's a new name each UTC day (`2026-11-24`) and knowing their ids says nothing. Chat, buzz, typing, files, letters. It's joined with a Trystero password, base32 HKDF-SHA256 of the secret (no salt, info `pigeon/pair/password`, 32 bytes), so offers can't be read and the room can't be entered without it. In the last hour of a UTC day tomorrow's room is joined too, and in the first hour yesterday's, so clocks less than an hour apart still meet; one pair session spans the rooms (`pairing/pair-rooms.ts`).
- `pair:<hash of both ids>`, the old pair room: base32 SHA-256 of `pigeon/pair/v1`, then the two ids sorted, newline-separated. Anyone who knows both ids can compute it, so it's only used to move friends from before pair secrets (see Pairing), and it stays the chat's id: pair envelopes and chat entries are signed for it in every pair room.
- `self:<daily name>`: where your own devices meet, from the self secret (see Devices).
- `link:<code>`: one per device link, used once (see Devices).
- `invite:<code>`: one per invite, used once for pairing.
- `page:<owner id>`: one per homepage. Snapshot requests and guestbook entries.
- `lobby`: opt-in public presence.

## Messages

Every message is an envelope `{ v: 1, from, room, at, body, sig }` in canonical JSON (keys sorted), at most 64 KB (files are chunked). `sig` is `from`'s base32 signature over the envelope without `sig`, so naming the room stops an envelope being replayed into another one. In pair rooms `room` is the chat's id (the old pair room's name) whichever daily room carried it, so a friend in two rooms at once sends the same envelope to both; `buzz`, `unfriend`, `pair-secret` and `pair-secret-ok` are taken once. Unsigned, oversized, malformed, wrong-room or blocked-sender messages are dropped, as is anything in a pair room from a key other than the friend's.

## Pairing

An invite is either a live code, below, or an invite link through Lighthouses (see Invite links). A code is an 8-character base32 code (40 bits, shown as `K7RT-VP2B`) that pairs once and is hosted for 30 minutes, also across reloads. It is shared typed, or as the link `https://pigeon.tomszir.com/#i/<code><fingerprint>`, which adds the inviter's fingerprint in base32 (23 characters after `#`); the QR code is the link. Typing accepts either case, with or without the dash, and reads 0, 1 and 8 as o, l and b. Opening a link takes it out of the address bar.

The inviter waits in `invite:<code>`. The invitee joins and sends `pair-request` (code, their name); the inviter answers `pair-offer` (code, its name). From a device that isn't its identity's first, either also carries `identity` and its device log, so the friend is saved as the identity and the fingerprint shown (and checked against a link's) is the identity's. The invitee is shown the name and fingerprint: from a link, an offer whose fingerprint doesn't match the link's is refused; from a typed code, they're warned to compare it with their friend's screen. Only when they choose Add does `pair-confirm` go back; the inviter adds them, makes a 32-byte pair secret, answers `pair-accept` with it (52 base32 characters), and stops hosting the code. Both then save each other as friends, with the secret, and meet in their pair room. Both meet only in the secret's rooms from the start and never join the old room, whose offers anyone who knows both ids could read. An invitee whose Pigeon was from before pair secrets ignores the secret, and the two have to pair again.

Friends from before pair secrets have no secret and meet in the old room. The first time both are there, the one with the lower id makes a secret (or uses the one it has) and sends `pair-secret`; the other keeps it, answers `pair-secret-ok`, and both join the secret's rooms and leave the old one for good. A friend who is in a secret's room has proved they hold it, which also counts as moving. Until a friend comes back, you keep waiting for them in the old room. Removing a friend keeps the secret with the farewell, so `unfriend` finds them wherever they are.

All rooms, pair and invite alike, can go through the TURN relay only (File, Always Go Through the Relay in the Messenger, or Connection in Settings; `iceTransportPolicy: "relay"`), so friends never see your IP address; it's slower and needs the relay to be reachable. It fails closed: with no relay to be had, Pigeon doesn't fall back to connecting directly, and a balloon (at most once a minute) says the relay can't be reached and where to turn the setting off.

While it waits, the invitee's Add a Friend shows each real step as it happens (`openInvite`'s `onStep`): reaching the relays (the room is joined), finding whoever made the code (a peer connected and was sent `pair-request`), and asking who they are (until `pair-offer`), then, after Add, waiting for `pair-accept`. A paper plane glides one way along a dotted path from you to your friend, its tip on the dots, fading in and out; the bar keeps moving, a countdown shows when it gives up (45 seconds for an answer, 20 for the accept), and a hint says what to check when a step stalls (`apps/messenger/connecting/`). Once paired, both sides go straight to the conversation, which opens with "Toms and Mika became friends on November 24 at 6:38 PM.", drawn from the friend's `addedAt`, so it stays on every visit.

### Invite links

An invite link waits at the inviter's Lighthouses, so it works while the inviter is offline, and lets in as many people as it was made for. It is `https://pigeon.tomszir.com/#a/<secret><fingerprint>`: a random 16-byte secret and the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of the inviter's identity, both base32 (52 characters), then `?l=<pigeon://lighthouse address>` when the inviter's list has none of the official Lighthouses. A link lasts 1 hour, 1 day, 7 days or 30 days and is for 1, 5, 10 or 25 people, chosen when it's made. Opening one in the address bar takes it out of the address. A link previews in a chat app as the site itself does, since nothing after the `#` reaches a server; it never names the inviter.

Making a link puts its card at each of the inviter's Lighthouses that has no pass, under `HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/invite/v1/card")` until it expires, with one drop hash, the link's own drop value's. The drop value never leaves that device. The card is `{ v: "pigeon/invite-card/v1", identity, deviceLog, keys, name, lighthouses, expires, open, from, tag, sig }`, `tag` being its own card tag, signed by the device that made the link, which must be active on the device log it carries, and sealed with AES-256-GCM under HKDF of the secret (info `pigeon/invite/key`). Whenever the inviter's device log, name or Lighthouses change, each open link's card is put again, and the old one dropped by its id at each Lighthouse; closing a link (cancelled, or used up) puts a card with `open` false.

Opening a link lists the card tag at the official Lighthouses and the one the link names, and opens up to 100 cards of at most 32 KB there, newest first: the identity's long fingerprint must be the link's, the signer active on the log (merged into the one this device keeps for that identity, if any), and each sealing key signed by its own device and active. Of the cards that hold, the one with the newest log wins (epoch, then entries), a closed one over an open one on the same log. It shows the name and fingerprint; asking puts a `mail:invite` letter sealed to the inviter's devices on the card, once, under `HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/invite/v1/ask/" + UTC date)` at the card's Lighthouses. Its body is the asker's name, device log, devices' sealing keys, at most two Lighthouses without passes (a Pigeon with none can't ask), `reply`, a random 16-byte secret kept for that link, and `link`, the link's card tag.

The device that made the link fetches requests under its ask tags (at most 16 KB each, 200 a check), and keeps each that names this link, from a device active on the log it carries and with keys signed by their devices: one per person, with each of their devices' reply secrets, until the inviter adds or declines it. With 50 waiting, another is answered `{ answer: "busy" }`, to ask again later. Requests from someone blocked are dropped; a request through a link that's closed or expired is answered `{ answer: "closed" }` (with `busy`, at most 20 times a link), and so is everyone still waiting when it closes; a device of someone already added gets the same `added` answer. An inviter who adds someone is their friend at once, so if the answer never reaches them (they stopped waiting, or stayed away past the link's end and two days more) the friend never shows up there. Adding keeps a new pair secret with the request first, so a Pigeon closed in between finishes it on its next start, then adds them as a friend with the request's device log, keys and Lighthouses, and puts, for each device that asked, a `mail:invite-answer` letter sealed to their devices under `HMAC-SHA256(reply, "pigeon/invite/v1/answer/" + UTC date)` at their Lighthouses: `{ answer: "added", secret, name, deviceLog }`, put again every 3 days until that device's receipt comes under the new pair secret. Declining puts `{ answer: "declined" }` once. The asker's device fetches its answer tags while the request waits, takes `added` from a device active on the log it carries, and saves the inviter as a friend with that secret; both then meet in the secret's rooms. While a link of yours is open or a request of yours waits, mail is looked for every 30 seconds instead of every 10 minutes.

Only the device that made a link answers through it. It tells your other devices its links (`{ secret, owner, expires, uses, used, open }`) in the self mailbox, and when a device takes the log that removes a link's owner, it puts a closed card for that link signed on the newer log, which wins over the owner's.

## Removing and blocking

Removing a friend deletes them and your chat from this browser and sends `unfriend` in your pair room. If they're offline, the id (and the pair secret) waits in `farewells` and a session of its own sends it the next time they're there. It's also left in their mailbox, as a `farewell` letter sealed to their devices still on their list, at their Lighthouses (the official ones if they never said): their Pigeon takes it the next time it fetches, only from a device on your list, and its receipt ends the wait as meeting them would. Their Pigeon keeps the chat read-only, marks them "removed you", and stops its session; removing them too deletes it. Pairing again makes you friends once more. Blocking also keeps their id in `blocked`: your invites leave their `pair-request` unanswered, and an invite of theirs you open is refused before anything is agreed. Petnames and mutes are yours alone and never leave the browser.

## Logs

Chats and guestbooks are signed append-only logs: each author appends entries with a sequence number (1, 2, 3...) and a Lamport timestamp; each entry is signed together with the log's id, which for a chat is the old pair room's name. On `hello` peers exchange `log-sync` summaries (per author, the highest sequence held with no gaps) and answer with `log-entries` batches of what the other lacks. Merging is a sorted union by (Lamport, author, sequence); an entry that doesn't verify, or isn't by one of the pair, is dropped, and the first entry seen for a sequence wins. A message is at most 4000 characters. A message written while the friend is offline goes by letter (see Letters), and also reaches them the next time you're both online. Receipts and deletions are entries too.

## Lighthouses and mail

A Lighthouse is a peer with an Ed25519 key, reached over WebSocket at an address `pigeon://lighthouse/<id>?url=...`; its `hello` carries `protocol` and `software`, shown as its own claim. A client's `hello` carries only `protocol` and a `nonce` chosen for the connection. Requests are numbered (`req`, from 1) and unsigned; each reply `{ v: 2, from, at, req, nonce, request, reply, sig }` is signed by the Lighthouse over the connection's nonce and `request`, the SHA-256 of the exact request frame it answers.

Each device's sealing key is bound to it as `{ device, key, sig }`: the raw P-256 public key in base64, signed by the device over `{ v: "pigeon/sealing-key/v1", device, key }`. Mail is a letter `{ v: "pigeon/letter/v1", room: "mail:<kind>", sender, to, date, id, head: { epoch, seq }, from, at, body, sig }`, signed by the device `from` of the identity `sender` (kinds so far: `farewell`, `self`, `receipt`, `invite`, `invite-answer`, `letter`; `id` 8 to 64 characters, taken once per sender). It's sealed with a random 32-byte content key (AES-256-GCM, nonce first), and the content key and that device's 32-byte drop value are wrapped for each recipient device under HKDF-SHA256 (no salt, info `pigeon/mail/wrap/<e>/<device key>`, both in base64) of ECDH between a fresh P-256 key `e` and the device's key. The blob is `{ v: 1, wraps: [{ e, w }], commit, content }` in canonical JSON: the wraps shuffled and naming no device, so a device tries each; `e` only as an uncompressed 65-byte point; `commit` HKDF of the content key (info `pigeon/mail/commit`), which every device checks, so all of them open the same letter. A device opens a letter only for its own identity, of a kind and date the mailbox it fetched from holds. A device whose sealing key isn't a usable point is left out of a blob, never the rest. Drop hashes go to `put` sorted. It's put under a tag, `HMAC-SHA256(pair or self secret, "pigeon/mail/v1/" + recipient identity + "/" + UTC date)`, with one drop hash per recipient device, for at most 30 days, at every Lighthouse on the recipient's list (said in `hello` as `lighthouses`, the official ones when unknown). Operations: `put`, `list` (exact tags and a cursor, answering id, tag and size), `get` (one blob), `drop`. A blob's id is the SHA-256 of its tag, blob and drops. Each device that takes mail in drops its copy and puts a signed `receipt` back; senders put again until each device's receipt comes. Mail counts only from a device active on the sender's newest log, and a farewell from a device since removed is undone.

A Pigeon looks for mail when it starts and every 10 minutes while it runs: at each of its Lighthouses, for every day since it last looked (30 at most), and tomorrow in the last two hours of today, it lists the tags each friend's mail to it, and your other devices', would be under, in lists of at most 512 tags, from its cursor; the cursor moves only past what it could get. It opens each letter, takes each (sender, id) once, drops its copy and puts a receipt back, again each time the same letter is put again, in case the last receipt was lost. A letter from a device it doesn't know on its sender's list is left, to be looked at again when it's put again. What it sent stays in its outbox, put again every 3 days, until every recipient device's receipt comes or 30 days pass; a letter that only expired was never heard, so a farewell goes on being said in person. Puts ask for an hour less than the most a Lighthouse keeps, so a clock that runs ahead of the Lighthouse's still fits.

## Letters

A message written while none of the friend's devices is connected goes by letter: a `mail:letter` sealed to their devices still on their list, under the pair secret's tag, at every Lighthouse on their list (the official ones when unknown). Its body is `{ log, entries }`: the chat's id and a batch of at most 50 (and 40 KB) of this device's own signed chat entries, the ones the friend isn't known to have. Each device keeps how far the friend has its entries: up to the last one sent while they stayed connected for 3 seconds after, all of them once one of their devices has synced (its `log-sync` is answered with whatever it lacks), or what receipted letters carried, one after another from the start (a receipt for a later letter doesn't vouch for an earlier one). A letter waits 5 seconds after the last message, so a burst goes as one; a friend going offline with entries they weren't seen to have sends those too, and so does each mail check, Pigeon's first included, for anything written before a close; a letter that expired unreceipted has its entries mailed again. One device of theirs syncing counts as all of them having it. Each letter waits for every recipient device's receipt and is put again every 3 days for at most 30 days, as all mail is; at most 50 wait per friend, counting only those not yet receipted, overtaken by a sync or expired. A Pigeon remembers its last 200 letters to a friend and the last 500 messages that came by letter, for the chat to say.

A Pigeon takes a letter from a device on the sender's list and only into the chat with them (one that comes before that chat is running is left, to be taken when it's put again), merging its entries with the checks `log-sync` uses (signed for this chat, by an author that counts, the first seen for a sequence winning), so a letter and a sync never disagree and arrival order doesn't matter; new entries count as unread. Each message remembers its way, which the chat says under it: sealing, left at which Lighthouses, not left yet when no Lighthouse took it (tried again at each check), received (once a receipt or a sync says so), or waiting until the friend is online when it can't be mailed; a message that came by letter says through which Lighthouse. Letters carry only the sending device's own entries; your other devices catch the chat up as before. Pigeons from before letters don't fetch them and get the messages when you're both online. Letters are sealed to devices' long-term sealing keys: there are no one-time prekeys yet.
