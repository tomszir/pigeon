# Protocol

How peers identify themselves, find each other, and exchange messages, letters and pages. Identity, envelopes, chat logs, pair sessions and pairing are implemented in `@pigeon/core` and run in the browser over Trystero. Sealing mail, the Lighthouse that holds it ([lighthouse.md](lighthouse.md)) and letters are built; files and pages are specified here but not built yet.

## Identity

- A peer is a WebCrypto signing keypair plus an ECDH P-256 keypair for sealing. `PeerId` is the base32 public signing key. New keys are Ed25519. Earlier Pigeons made an ECDSA P-256 key where the browser lacked Ed25519; those identities keep working, but no new P-256 key is made. A browser without Ed25519 (older than Chrome and Edge 137, Firefox 129, Safari 17) is told it's too old to make an identity, and nothing is stored.
- The keypair is made on the first visit and kept in IndexedDB; the private key is non-extractable, so not even the page can read it. Clearing site data is a new identity.
- A `PeerId` is 52 base32 characters for an Ed25519 key (32 bytes) or 104 for a raw P-256 point (65 bytes); the length says which. Core never holds keys: it signs through a `Signer` and verifies and hashes through `Crypto`, which the platform implements.
- Signatures, in envelopes, chat and device logs, mail and Lighthouse replies, are lowercase base32. One written in capitals is refused even though its bytes verify, because signatures are kept and compared as text, and a second spelling would count as a second signature.
- For the same reason a P-256 signature is low-S: the raw `r || s` (64 bytes) with `s` at most n / 2. Anyone holding an ECDSA signature can make `r || n - s`, which verifies just as well, so every `Crypto` refuses the high-S spelling before verifying, and Pigeon signs low-S. Earlier Pigeons signed either way, and their signatures are still read where nobody can swap them: a device-log entry whose spelling the next entry's signed `prev` names, and what a device already keeps (its device logs, a link bundle, an export file), whose remaining high-S signatures it reads in their low-S spelling. A chat entry is sent in its low-S spelling, in a sync or a letter, so an old message still reaches a device that lacks it; an invite request kept until you press Add is read as kept data too, and every device puts its invite links' cards again once, open and closed, signed low-S, since a card carries your device log too. A letter the outbox puts again is read the same way: the device logs, chat entries and sealing keys in its body go out low-S. Anything else signed high-S, such as a letter put before the update and not yet put again, is refused. An Ed25519 signature has one spelling when its S is below the group order, as RFC 8032 requires, and every `Crypto` checks that too, as it checks that a P-256 `r` and `s` are in range.
- Handles are self-chosen, not unique, at most 32 characters. A name you choose may not hold control characters (line breaks among them), line or paragraph separators, or the marks that reorder text (U+061C, U+200E, U+200F, U+202A to U+202E, U+2066 to U+2069). A name that arrives with them, in `hello`, a profile, pairing, a device link, your devices' mail or an invite, has them taken out once its signature is checked; a name with nothing else left shows as "Friend", as does a friend's saved one; an invite or a profile change naming nobody is refused, and a saved name of yours with nothing left is asked for again. An older Pigeon may have let such a name through, so taking them out rather than refusing keeps that person reachable, and a stored name of yours, a friend's or a petname is shown with them taken out too. Petnames are what you call a contact. Pairing shows a 16-hex-character fingerprint (the first 8 bytes of the id's SHA-256, spelled in fours) to compare.
- Everyone's picture is a pixel pigeon drawn from their id ([design.md](design.md#avatars)). `hello` says which version of the drawing to use, so a newer version never changes the bird of someone who already has one.
- Each device gets a sealing (ECDH P-256) keypair, announced in `hello` and bound to the device by its own signature, so mail, invites and letters can be sealed to devices. `@pigeon/core` seals and opens mail; announcing the key in `hello` isn't built yet.

## Devices

One person can use Pigeon on several devices. Each device has its own non-extractable key, and an identity is a device log (`devices/device-log.ts`). The first device's key starts the log and stays the identity's `PeerId`, so friends, fingerprints and avatars never change; it's on the list without an entry.

One device writes the log at a time: the first device, until a recovery key names another (below). Entries `{ seq, prev, kind, device, name?, at, sig }` are signed by the writer over the entry and the identity, each naming the signature of the one before: `add` puts a device on, named; `remove` takes one off for good within its epoch (its key can't be added again, and the writer can't be removed); `name` renames one. With one writer each epoch's entries are a single chain, and a copy further along it replaces a shorter one; a copy that disagrees on an entry both have is ignored. Two copies of an entry agree when their signed content and their signatures' low-S spellings are the same, so a second spelling of a P-256 signature can't split a chain, and the last entry of every chain is low-S. A log holds at most 100 entries across all its epochs. When a merge would hold more, the oldest epochs lose their last entries first, and the current epoch is never cut. No other device can change the list, so a stolen second device can do nothing to it.

A recovery key outranks every device. It's an Ed25519 key whose public half the first device commits to once, as `recovery: { key, at, sig }`, signed over `{ identity, type: "recovery-key", key, at }`. Where two copies of a log name different keys, the one you had first stays. A recovery is an item of `epochs`: `{ recover, entries }`, where `recover` is `{ epoch, writer, keep: [{ device, name }], next, at, sig }`, signed by the key current before it (`recovery.key` for epoch 1, the previous `next` after) over `{ identity, type: "recover", ... }`. Its `keep` (up to 8 devices, the writer among them) is the list from then on, and every other device is removed as of its `at`. The writer signs that epoch's entries, whose signed bytes add `epoch`. Each epoch stands on its own: its recover's signature and its own chain decide whether it holds, and the list comes from the latest epoch alone. An older epoch's entries only say who was once yours and until when, so a stolen device that writes more of them after a recovery changes nothing and can't make a log fail. Where two copies disagree on an epoch's recover, the one you hold stays if you hold a later epoch on top of it, so a used and replaced key can't start its epoch again. At your latest epoch the lower signature wins, with everything after it. Copies that agree take the one with more epochs, and within an epoch the longer copy of the same chain wins. Committing a key also signs a `name` entry for the first device, so a Pigeon from before, which takes a log only when it has more entries and then keeps it whole, keeps the commitment too. Epoch 0's entries and their bytes are unchanged, so Pigeons from before read them and ignore `recovery` and `epochs`. A log holds up to 8 epochs.

A recovered device's log is a tail: `from` is its first epoch, `by` the key that signed its recover, and it has nothing before that. Only a Pigeon that already has your earlier epochs takes a tail, by checking it follows on from them, and the tail's own device takes a whole log only if that log's key for its first epoch is `by`. Pairing checks whoever answers against the log you already keep for them, loaded from storage if need be. That turns away a tail from anyone you don't know, an old copy that would undo a recovery you've seen, and a device (a stolen first device, say) that speaks for itself when your copy of its log says a recovery took it off the list. A friend who keeps a log with epochs sends it back to its owner in their pair room, so a recovered device gets its whole log from the first friend it pairs with again. That friend's Pigeon knows them already, so pairing again keeps the chat and petname, takes the new pair secret and moves to its rooms.

Envelopes and chat entries are signed by a device. In a pair room a friend takes envelopes from any device active on your log, as theirs is to you, and chat log authors are devices, so sequence numbers never collide; presence shows the newest sent. A removed device's entries from before its removal (by their time, which is the device's own word) still count, so a device linked later catches up with no gaps; nothing later is taken from it. A device that finds itself removed stops talking. Your own other devices in the same pair room catch the chat up with each other (`log-sync`, `log-entries`) but aren't the friend. An identity with a log sends it as `device-log` before `hello`, in every pair room and again whenever it changes; friends keep it under `devices:<id>`. `hello` carries `devices: true`, and a friend whose `hello` lacks it is on a Pigeon from before device logs: it hears only your first device, and a second device's chat says so until they update.

A copied browser profile holds the same device key as the original. Every `hello` carries `instance`, 10 random bytes in base32 made each time the Messenger starts, and a device that hears a hello signed by its own key, bound to the connection it came on, with another `instance` knows it's open in another browser too. Only one tab per browser runs the Messenger, so a second tab is never mistaken for a copy.

Linking is in person, from the first device. It hosts a live code like an invite's (see Live codes) in `link/v2:<nameplate>` and the link's own room, for 10 minutes, and shows it in letters and as the link `#l/<secret><fingerprint>` in a QR code, with this Pigeon's fingerprint. The new device, which must have no friends or devices of its own (a Pigeon that has them can leave first, telling its friends and forgetting everything), opens it (from the link, it first asks you to choose Link, since anyone can send one) and runs the live-code handshake; the existing device's `link-offer` carries its key confirmation and whose it is (and its device log, if it isn't the first device). From a link, an offer whose fingerprint isn't the link's is refused; from a typed code, the new device shows the identity's fingerprint to compare with the one Link a Device shows, and asks only after Continue. It asks with `link-request`, carrying its device's sealing key, which must be a key mail can be sealed to. Both then show the new device's fingerprint, which the handshake covered, so the device asking is the one in your hand; Not This Device refuses it and leaves the code open for yours. A code with a request showing at its 10 minutes ends when that request is answered or goes. Confirming on the first device signs `add` and sends `link-accept` with a bundle sealed to that key (refused beforehand, with nothing signed, when it wouldn't fit one sealed letter, 44 KB; nothing is signed for a device that has already left, and one that leaves after the `add` stays on the list, to be handed everything if it's linked again or removed in My Devices, since a removal is final): the identity, the device log, the self secret, the profile, the friends (with their pair secrets), the blocked list and friends' device logs. Chats aren't in it; they catch up through pair rooms. The new device keeps it all and starts again as that identity. The profile is copied then; afterwards your device log and your name and personal message are kept in step through the self room and the self mailbox, and your Lighthouse list through the self mailbox; the rest of your own data isn't kept in step between devices.

Your devices meet in `self:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(self secret, "pigeon/self/v1/" + UTC date)>`, locked with HKDF of the self secret (info `pigeon/self/password`) and renamed daily like pair rooms. Device logs travel there, so a removal reaches your other devices with no friend online, and so does `profile`, your name and personal message with when they last changed; a device takes one newer than its own. A device that finds itself removed stops talking and says so.

The self mailbox carries the same to a device that's offline, and your Lighthouse list too: a `mail:self` letter, sealed to each of your other devices still on your list, under the self secret's tags, at every Lighthouse on this device's list and on the list your other devices last said in the self room. Its body is a snapshot, `{ log, keys, profile?, lighthouses?, invites? }`: your device log, the sealing keys this device knows for your devices (its own included), and your name and your Lighthouse list (`{ list, changedAt }`), each with when it last changed, and the invite links this device made (see Invite links). It's sent when a change is made on this device (the list, a device's name, the recovery key, a link, your name or your Lighthouses) and when a device's sealing key is heard for the first time, so a device linked while another was away can be written to. A new one takes the place of an older one still waiting on the same devices, so a device that's away has one snapshot waiting, not one per change. A device fetching mail opens everything at all its Lighthouses before acting on any of it, and merges the device log of every `mail:self` letter first, whoever it came from, since a log is signed by whoever wrote it; the rest of a letter counts only from a device on the list that results, so a removal counts whichever letter was listed first. Each key counts only when signed by its own device and that device is on the list; the name and the list are taken when newer than this device's own and no more than a day ahead of its clock. A Lighthouse list kept from before devices shared theirs counts as changed when the Pigeon first starts with this, and is sent then. A device a removal takes off the list, or a recovery leaves off, is sent a letter of its own holding only the device log, so it hears and stops talking even if it never meets another of your devices.

## Your data in a file

Export My Data writes a `.txt` whose sealed line is `pigeon-sealed:v1:<rounds>:<salt>:<iv>:<sealed>` (base64), made with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 rounds) and AES-256-GCM from a passphrase. Inside it is the canonical JSON `{ kind: "pigeon-export", v: 1, at, self, profile, friends, blocked, friendDevices, logs, settings }`, where `logs` maps each friend to their chat log as stored. It also holds each friend's pair secret, so whoever has the file and its passphrase can find and enter your pair rooms and fetch the sealed mail under your mail tags: there they learn when a friend is online, their name, profile and devices, and their IP address unless they always go through the relay, though without a device key they can't read or send messages. It holds no private keys, and not your own device log or self secret. Import refuses a file whose `self` isn't this device's identity, merges each chat's entries with the same checks a catch-up uses, adds friends and blocks this device lacks (keeping its own where both have one), takes the profile and settings only on a device with no name yet, and starts the Messenger again.

## Rooms

All traffic goes through the `Transport` interface. Messages to one peer arrive in the order they were sent, whatever their size.

Inside a room the transport names connections (`ConnectionId`); those are not identities. A connection becomes a friend only by sending an envelope signed by that friend's key, starting with `hello`.

A room also says which channel each connection is: `binding(peer)` gives `{ local, remote }`, SHA-256 certificate fingerprints as lowercase hex with no colons. `local` is from this side's own description's `a=fingerprint`; `remote` is the SHA-256 of the certificate the data channel's DTLS transport checked (`getRemoteCertificates()`), not the remote description, since a peer can send a new description after the handshake that some browsers show as current while the old channel runs on. It's nothing when there's no direct peer connection (a peer carried through other peers), the channel isn't connected, the browser can't say which certificate DTLS checked, a description names anything but exactly one certificate (any `a=fingerprint` line that isn't `sha-256` in that form, or two different ones), or the remote description names another certificate than DTLS checked; then the connection can't be bound. Whoever writes into the channel holds the key of the certificate DTLS checked.

In pair and self rooms a connection counts as a device only once that device has signed its channel. On every new connection each side sends its device logs and then `nonce`, 16 random bytes in base32, answering a nonce signed by a key it takes there (the friend's devices, your own, or this device's own key), and each one once (at most 8 on a connection), since a peer that reconnects keeping its id greets again. The answer is `hello` with `binding: { nonce, local, remote }`, the nonce and the fingerprints as the sender sees them, its own first; the receiver takes it only when the nonce is the one it sent on that connection and the fingerprints are its own view swapped, compared as bytes. A hello forwarded or replayed from another connection names another channel and is ignored. Everything else counts only on a connection proven that way, and only from the device that proved it, so a replay has no connection to count on; presence, profile and `log-sync` go to a connection once it has proved itself, and nothing more goes to it once it has left, not even what was queued for it before. What it sent before it left still counts when it's read later (its last messages, an `unfriend`), its hello included, which still proves what came after it; but nothing that would answer it or change who it is (`bye`, `log-sync`), and nothing sent under its id afterwards counts as it. A Pigeon from before binding sends hellos without it and never answers a nonce: the two show each other offline and meet only through Lighthouses (letters, farewells, the self mailbox) until it updates. A friend with an updated device online gets no letters, so their devices not yet updated catch up once they are ([flows.md](flows.md#saying-hello)).

- `pair:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/pair/v2/" + UTC date)>`: where two friends meet, named from the 256-bit pair secret only they hold, so it's a new name each UTC day (`2026-11-24`) and knowing their ids says nothing. Chat, buzz, typing, files, letters. It's joined with a Trystero password, base32 HKDF-SHA256 of the secret (no salt, info `pigeon/pair/password`, 32 bytes), so offers can't be read and the room can't be entered without it. In the last hour of a UTC day tomorrow's room is joined too, and in the first hour yesterday's, so clocks less than an hour apart still meet; one pair session spans the rooms (`pairing/pair-rooms.ts`).
- `pair:<hash of both ids>`, the old pair room: base32 SHA-256 of `pigeon/pair/v1`, then the two ids sorted, newline-separated. Anyone who knows both ids can compute it, so it's only used to move friends from before pair secrets (see Pairing), and it stays the chat's id: pair envelopes and chat entries are signed for it in every pair room.
- `self:<daily name>`: where your own devices meet, from the self secret (see Devices).
- `link/v2:<nameplate>` and `link/v2:<hash of the link's secret>`: one pair per device-link code, used once (see Devices and Live codes).
- `invite/v2:<nameplate>` and `invite/v2:<hash of the link's secret>`: one pair per live invite code, used once for pairing (see Live codes).
- `page:<owner id>`: one per homepage. Snapshot requests and guestbook entries.
- `lobby`: opt-in public presence.

## Messages

Every message is an envelope `{ v: 1, from, room, at, body, sig }` in canonical JSON (keys sorted), at most 64 KB (files are chunked). `sig` is `from`'s base32 signature over the envelope without `sig`, so naming the room stops an envelope being replayed into another one; in pair and self rooms a bound `hello` (see Rooms) ties a connection to its device, which stops one being replayed on another connection. In pair rooms `room` is the chat's id (the old pair room's name) whichever daily room carried it, so a friend in two rooms at once sends the same envelope to both; `buzz`, `unfriend`, `pair-secret` and `pair-secret-ok` are taken once. Unsigned, oversized, malformed, wrong-room or blocked-sender messages are dropped, as is anything in a pair room from a key other than the friend's.

## Pairing

An invite is either a live code, below, or an invite link through Lighthouses (see Invite links). A live code pairs once and is hosted for 30 minutes, also across reloads.

### Live codes

Invite codes and device-link codes work the same way. A code is 10 base32 characters, shown `K7RT-VP2-BXQ`: the first 4 are the nameplate, the last 6 the password. The nameplate names the room, `invite/v2:<nameplate>` or `link/v2:<nameplate>`, and is public: the relays see the room's name. The password only goes into SPAKE2, so nothing the relays see can test a guess. Typing accepts either case, with or without the dashes, and reads 0, 1 and 8 as o, l and b. A code is also shown as a link, `https://pigeon.tomszir.com/#i/<secret><fingerprint>` (`#l/` for a device link): a random 16-byte secret in base32 that takes the password's place, and the 8-byte fingerprint of the identity that made it, 39 characters after `i/`. A link meets in a room of its own, `invite/v2:` (or `link/v2:`) and the first 16 bytes, in base32, of SHA-256 of `pigeon/invite/v2/room/<secret>` (`pigeon/link/v2/room/...`), so nobody without the link can find it; the side that made the code waits in both rooms, each taking only its own kind of secret. The QR code is the link. Opening a link takes it out of the address bar.

The side that opened the code (`a`) and the side that made it (`b`) run SPAKE2 (RustCrypto's `spake2` crate, Ed25519 group, built to WebAssembly) on each connection in the room. The password is `code:<password>` or `link:<secret>` as UTF-8, and the identities are `pigeon/invite/v2/a/<room>` and `pigeon/invite/v2/b/<room>` (`pigeon/link/v2/...` for device links). `a` sends `pake-start` (`via`: `code` or `link`, and its message), `b` answers `pake-reply`, and both get a key. Each side's confirmation is HMAC-SHA256, under HKDF-SHA256 of that key (info `pigeon/invite/v2/confirm/a` or `/b`), over the transcript in canonical JSON: `{ v: "pigeon/invite/v2", room, via, a, b, aDevice, bDevice, aDtls, bDtls }`, both SPAKE2 messages in base64, both device keys (each the signer of that side's envelopes), and both DTLS fingerprints from `binding(peer)`. `a` sends its confirmation in `pake-prove`; `b` checks it first, and a wrong one is a wrong try, answered `pake-wrong`; so is a run not proven within 30 seconds, or whose connection leaves before proving. Five wrong tries stop the code, counted across both rooms and across reloads, and once they're used no confirmation that came at the same time is checked. `b` keeps at most 16 runs going in a room at once. `a` runs at most 4 at once and 8 in all each time it opens a code, a stalled one giving way after 15 seconds, since whoever answers `a` can test one guess against its confirmation. `pake-wrong` isn't proof of anything, so a Pigeon that gets it says the one that answered calls the code wrong. `b`'s confirmation comes in its offer. Someone in the middle shows each side a certificate of their own, so the transcripts differ and neither confirmation holds; every later message must come over the same connection, signed by the same device. A transport that can't say a connection's fingerprints can't run it.

The inviter's `pair-offer` carries its confirmation and name; from a device that isn't its identity's first, it also carries `identity` and its device log, so the friend is saved as the identity and the fingerprint shown (and checked against a link's) is the identity's. The invitee is shown the name and fingerprint: from a link, an offer whose fingerprint doesn't match the link's is refused; from a typed code, they're warned to compare it with their friend's screen. Only when they choose Ask to Be Friends does `pair-request` go back, with their name, device log and their device's sealing key, signed by that device. The inviter sees each request in Add a Friend, with the name, fingerprint and bird (and a balloon when Add a Friend isn't the window in front), and chooses Add or Decline. Add makes a 32-byte pair secret and answers `pair-accept`: a `mail:pair` letter sealed to that sealing key, its body `{ session, value }`, `session` the SHA-256 of the transcript in hex and `value` the secret, so it counts only on that connection. Then the inviter stops hosting the code. Decline answers `pair-decline` and leaves the code open. Both then save each other as friends, with the secret, and meet only in the secret's rooms, never the old room named from their ids. Pigeons from before live codes went through SPAKE2 can't pair or link with ones after: they meet in other rooms. A current Pigeon tells an older one's 8-character code, and its `#i/` or `#l/` link of 21 characters, from a mistyped one, and says to have the other side reload Pigeon for a new code; anything else that doesn't read as a code is just refused.

All rooms, pair and invite alike, can go through the TURN relay only (File, Always Go Through the Relay in the Messenger, or Connection in Settings; `iceTransportPolicy: "relay"`), so friends never see your IP address; it's slower and needs the relay to be reachable. With it on, mail goes only to Lighthouses you chose (see Lighthouses you didn't choose), since a friend's own Lighthouse would see the IP the relay hides. Turning it on or off joins every room Pigeon hosts again under the new setting: friends', the code you host, your own devices' room, and a device link being shown, which gets a new code. A device link another device has already answered is left to finish, and an invite or link you're opening keeps the setting it was opened with until it ends, as both have already reached the other side. It fails closed: with no relay to be had, Pigeon doesn't fall back to connecting directly, and a balloon (at most once a minute) says the relay can't be reached and where to turn the setting off.

While it waits, the invitee's Add a Friend shows each real step as it happens (`openInvite`'s `onStep`): reaching the relays (the room is joined), finding whoever made the code (a peer connected and was sent `pake-start`), and asking who they are (until `pair-offer`), then, after Ask to Be Friends, waiting for the inviter to add them. A paper plane glides one way along a dotted path from you to your friend, its tip on the dots, fading in and out; the bar keeps moving, a countdown shows when it gives up (45 seconds for an answer, 3 minutes for the inviter to add you), and a hint says what to check when a step stalls (`apps/messenger/connecting/`). Once paired, both sides go straight to the conversation, which opens with "Toms and Mika became friends on November 24 at 6:38 PM.", drawn from the friend's `addedAt`, so it stays on every visit.

### Invite links

An invite link waits at the inviter's Lighthouses, so it works while the inviter is offline, and lets in as many people as it was made for. It is `https://pigeon.tomszir.com/#a/<secret><fingerprint>`: a random 16-byte secret and the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of the inviter's identity, both base32 (52 characters), then `?l=<pigeon://lighthouse address>` when the inviter's list has none of the official Lighthouses. A link lasts 1 hour, 1 day, 7 days or 30 days and is for 1, 5, 10 or 25 people, chosen when it's made. Opening one in the address bar takes it out of the address. A link previews in a chat app as the site itself does, since nothing after the `#` reaches a server; it never names the inviter.

Making a link puts its card at each of the inviter's Lighthouses that has no pass, under `HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/invite/v1/card")` until it expires, with one drop hash, the link's own drop value's. The drop value never leaves that device. The card is `{ v: "pigeon/invite-card/v1", identity, deviceLog, keys, name, lighthouses, expires, open, from, tag, sig }`, `tag` being its own card tag, signed by the device that made the link, which must be active on the device log it carries, and sealed with AES-256-GCM under HKDF of the secret (info `pigeon/invite/key`). Whenever the inviter's device log, name or Lighthouses change, each open link's card is put again, and the old one dropped by its id at each Lighthouse; closing a link (cancelled, or used up) puts a card with `open` false.

Opening a link lists the card tag at the official Lighthouses and the one the link names (when this Pigeon may reach it: see Lighthouses you didn't choose), and opens up to 100 cards of at most 32 KB there, newest first: the identity's long fingerprint must be the link's, the signer active on the log (merged into the one this device keeps for that identity, if any), and each sealing key signed by its own device and active. Of the cards that hold, the one with the newest log wins (epoch, then entries), a closed one over an open one on the same log. It shows the name and fingerprint; asking puts a `mail:invite` letter sealed to the inviter's devices on the card, once, under `HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/invite/v1/ask/" + UTC date)` at the card's Lighthouses. Its body is the asker's name, device log, devices' sealing keys, at most two Lighthouses without passes (a Pigeon with none can't ask), `reply`, a random 16-byte secret kept for that link, and `link`, the link's card tag.

The device that made the link fetches requests under its ask tags (at most 16 KB each, 200 a check), and keeps each that names this link, from a device active on the log it carries and with keys signed by their devices: one per person, with each of their devices' reply secrets, until the inviter adds or declines it. With 50 waiting, another is answered `{ answer: "busy" }`, to ask again later. Requests from someone blocked are dropped; a request through a link that's closed or expired is answered `{ answer: "closed" }` (with `busy`, at most 20 times a link), and so is everyone still waiting when it closes; a device of someone already added gets the same `added` answer. An inviter who adds someone is their friend at once, so if the answer never reaches them (they stopped waiting, or stayed away past the link's end and two days more) the friend never shows up there. Adding keeps a new pair secret with the request first, so a Pigeon closed in between finishes it on its next start, then adds them as a friend with the request's device log, keys and Lighthouses, and puts, for each device that asked, a `mail:invite-answer` letter sealed to their devices under `HMAC-SHA256(reply, "pigeon/invite/v1/answer/" + UTC date)` at their Lighthouses: `{ answer: "added", secret, name, deviceLog }`, put again every 3 days until that device's receipt comes under the new pair secret. Declining puts `{ answer: "declined" }` once. The asker's device fetches its answer tags while the request waits, takes `added` from a device active on the log it carries, and saves the inviter as a friend with that secret; both then meet in the secret's rooms. While a link of yours is open or a request of yours waits, mail is looked for every 30 seconds instead of every 10 minutes.

Only the device that made a link answers through it. It tells your other devices its links (`{ secret, owner, expires, uses, used, open }`) in the self mailbox, and when a device takes the log that removes a link's owner, it puts a closed card for that link signed on the newer log, which wins over the owner's.

## Removing and blocking

Removing a friend deletes them and your chat from this browser and sends `unfriend` in your pair room. If they're offline, the id (and the pair secret) waits in `farewells` and a session of its own sends it the next time they're there. It's also left in their mailbox, as a `farewell` letter sealed to their devices still on their list, at their Lighthouses (the official ones if they never said): their Pigeon takes it the next time it fetches, only from a device on your list, and its receipt ends the wait as meeting them would. Their Pigeon keeps the chat read-only, marks them "removed you", and stops its session; removing them too deletes it. Pairing again makes you friends once more. Blocking also keeps their id in `blocked`: your codes leave their `pair-request` unanswered and never show it, and an invite of theirs you open is refused before anything is agreed. Petnames and mutes are yours alone and never leave the browser.

## Logs

Chats and guestbooks are signed append-only logs: each author appends entries with a sequence number (1, 2, 3...) and a Lamport timestamp; each entry is signed together with the log's id, which for a chat is the old pair room's name. On `hello` peers exchange `log-sync` summaries (per author, the highest sequence held with no gaps) and answer with `log-entries` batches of what the other lacks. Merging is a sorted union by (Lamport, author, sequence); an entry that doesn't verify, or isn't by one of the pair, is dropped, and the first entry seen for a sequence wins. Any entry that verifies is taken, whatever its Lamport time, as long as it's a safe integer; a time past that is never offered in a catch-up. A new entry's time is one past the highest held that is no more than 2^20 times its rank (how many entries sort at or below it), and never past the largest safe integer (2^53 - 1): a made-up time far ahead is held and shown, but the clock doesn't follow it, so it can't push later messages past what peers take. A message is at most 4000 characters. A message written while the friend is offline goes by letter (see Letters), and also reaches them the next time you're both online. Receipts and deletions are entries too.

## Lighthouses and mail

A Lighthouse is a peer with an Ed25519 key, reached over WebSocket at an address `pigeon://lighthouse/<id>?url=...`; its `hello` carries `protocol` and `software`, shown as its own claim. A client's `hello` carries only `protocol` and a `nonce` chosen for the connection. Requests are numbered (`req`, from 1) and unsigned; each reply `{ v: 2, from, at, req, nonce, request, reply, sig }` is signed by the Lighthouse over the connection's nonce and `request`, the SHA-256 of the exact request frame it answers.

Each device's sealing key is bound to it as `{ device, key, sig }`: the raw P-256 public key in base64, signed by the device over `{ v: "pigeon/sealing-key/v1", device, key }`. Mail is a letter `{ v: "pigeon/letter/v1", room: "mail:<kind>", sender, to, date, id, head: { epoch, seq }, from, at, body, sig }`, signed by the device `from` of the identity `sender` (kinds so far: `farewell`, `self`, `receipt`, `invite`, `invite-answer`, `letter`, and `pair` and `link`, a live code's answer, handed over the connection and never left at a Lighthouse; `id` 8 to 64 characters, taken once per sender). It's sealed with a random 32-byte content key (AES-256-GCM, nonce first), and the content key and that device's 32-byte drop value are wrapped for each recipient device under HKDF-SHA256 (no salt, info `pigeon/mail/wrap/<e>/<device key>`, both in base64) of ECDH between a fresh P-256 key `e` and the device's key. The blob is `{ v: 1, wraps: [{ e, w }], commit, content }` in canonical JSON: the wraps shuffled and naming no device, so a device tries each; `e` only as an uncompressed 65-byte point; `commit` HKDF of the content key (info `pigeon/mail/commit`), which every device checks, so all of them open the same letter. A device opens a letter only for its own identity, of a kind and date the mailbox it fetched from holds. A device whose sealing key isn't a usable point is left out of a blob, never the rest. Drop hashes go to `put` sorted. It's put under a tag, `HMAC-SHA256(pair or self secret, "pigeon/mail/v1/" + recipient identity + "/" + UTC date)`, with one drop hash per recipient device, for at most 30 days, at every Lighthouse on the recipient's list (said in `hello` as `lighthouses`, the official ones when unknown). Operations: `put`, `list` (exact tags and a cursor, answering id, tag and size), `get` (one blob), `drop`. A blob's id is the SHA-256 of its tag, blob and drops. Each device that takes mail in drops its copy and puts a signed `receipt` back; senders put again until each device's receipt comes. Mail counts only from a device active on the sender's newest log, and a farewell from a device since removed is undone.

### Lighthouses you didn't choose

A Lighthouse sees the IP address of whoever connects to it, and connecting points your browser at its host. Lighthouses you chose are on your list, on the list your other devices last said, or official; any other comes from someone else: a friend's `hello`, an invite link's `?l=`, the card behind a link, or a request through one. Pigeon connects to one someone else named only when its URL is `wss://` to a public host, and never while Always Go Through the Relay is on. Checking a Lighthouse in Network, one on your list or one you're adding, reaches it whatever its address, since you're the one choosing it. A host isn't public when it's loopback, private, link-local, shared (100.64.0.0/10), multicast or another special-use address, IPv4 or IPv6 (IPv4-mapped and NAT64 forms included), `localhost`, a single-label name, or a name under `.local`, `.localdomain`, `.internal`, `.intranet`, `.home.arpa`, `.home`, `.lan`, `.corp`, `.private`, `.test`, `.invalid`, `.example`, `.onion` or `.alt`; an IPv4 host is read as a browser reads it, so `127.1` or `2130706433` is 127.0.0.1. An address someone else names counts as one you chose only when its URL is exactly the same as yours: another path on the same host can be some other service behind it, run by someone else. A name is taken as written, so one whose DNS answers with a private address (`127.0.0.1.nip.io`, say) isn't caught. Only some browsers check that themselves (recent Chrome asks before a public page reaches the local network; Firefox and Safari don't for WebSockets), so with relay-only off a friend can still point a `wss://` connection at your network that way; it opens with TLS, so a service there sees a TLS handshake, not a WebSocket frame. Mail for a friend goes to the Lighthouses of theirs Pigeon may reach; with none, it waits until you meet, and the chat says so, while your other devices still get their copy of what you wrote. Receipts are mail too: one for a friend's letter goes only where Pigeon may reach, so with relay-only on and a friend who uses only their own Lighthouse, their chat doesn't hear you have it, and their Pigeon puts the letter again every 3 days until you meet or 30 days pass. Turning relay-only on closes any connection open to a Lighthouse it now refuses. A request through a link that none of the card's Lighthouses Pigeon may reach can take isn't sent, and Add a Friend says it couldn't be left. On the inviter's side, adding someone whose Lighthouses it may not reach adds them, but the answer waits: it's put again every 3 days, so they hear once relay-only is off, or never if it stays on.

A Pigeon looks for mail when it starts and every 10 minutes while it runs: at each of its Lighthouses, for every day since it last looked (30 at most), and tomorrow in the last two hours of today, it lists the tags each friend's mail to it, and your other devices', would be under, in lists of at most 512 tags, from its cursor; the cursor moves only past what it could get. It opens each letter, takes each (sender, id) once, drops its copy and puts a receipt back, again each time the same letter is put again, in case the last receipt was lost. A letter from a device it doesn't know on its sender's list is left, to be looked at again when it's put again. What it sent stays in its outbox, put again every 3 days, until every recipient device's receipt comes or 30 days pass; a letter that only expired was never heard, so a farewell goes on being said in person. Puts ask for an hour less than the most a Lighthouse keeps, so a clock that runs ahead of the Lighthouse's still fits.

## Letters

A message written while none of the friend's devices is connected goes by letter: a `mail:letter` sealed to their devices still on their list, under the pair secret's tag, at every Lighthouse on their list (the official ones when unknown). Its body is `{ log, entries }`: the chat's id and a batch of at most 50 (and 40 KB) of this device's own signed chat entries, the ones the friend isn't known to have. Each device keeps how far the friend has its entries: up to the last one sent while they stayed connected for 3 seconds after, all of them once one of their devices has synced (its `log-sync` is answered with whatever it lacks), or what receipted letters carried, one after another from the start (a receipt for a later letter doesn't vouch for an earlier one). A letter waits 5 seconds after the last message, so a burst goes as one; a friend going offline with entries they weren't seen to have sends those too, and so does each mail check, Pigeon's first included, for anything written before a close; a letter that expired unreceipted has its entries mailed again. One device of theirs syncing counts as all of them having it. Each letter waits for every recipient device's receipt and is put again every 3 days for at most 30 days, as all mail is; at most 50 wait per friend, counting only those not yet receipted, overtaken by a sync or expired. A Pigeon remembers its last 200 letters to a friend and the last 500 messages that came by letter, for the chat to say.

A Pigeon takes a letter from a device on the sender's list and only into the chat with them (one that comes before that chat is running is left, to be taken when it's put again), merging its entries with the checks `log-sync` uses (signed for this chat, by an author that counts, the first seen for a sequence winning), so a letter and a sync never disagree and arrival order doesn't matter; new entries count as unread. Each message remembers its way, which the chat says under it: sealing, left at which Lighthouses, not left yet when no Lighthouse took it (tried again at each check), received (once a receipt or a sync says so), or waiting until the friend is online when it can't be mailed; a message that came by letter says through which Lighthouse. Letters carry only the sending device's own entries. Each goes with a copy for your other devices: the same `mail:letter`, from and to your own identity, with `friend` in its body, sealed to your other devices still on your list under the self secret's tags, at the Lighthouses self mail goes to. A device takes a copy only from another of your devices, into the chat with that friend if it has one, as its own words: not as unread, and with no "came by letter" line. Pigeons from before letters don't fetch them and get the messages when you're both online. Letters are sealed to devices' long-term sealing keys: there are no one-time prekeys yet.

## pigeon:// addresses

`pigeon://<name>/<path>`. The name resolves petname → handle seen signed by the Lighthouse directory → raw `PeerId`; ambiguity shows a picker. Explorer joins `page:<owner>`, accepts the highest-versioned snapshot with a valid owner signature, sanitizes it with [PigeonHTML](pigeonhtml.md), and renders it. No answer: the "page not hosted" screen.
