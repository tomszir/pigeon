# Protocol

How peers identify themselves, find each other, and exchange messages, letters and pages. Identity, envelopes, chat logs, pair sessions and pairing are implemented in `@pigeon/core` and run in the browser over Trystero (M2); files, letters and pages are the design the code will follow.

## Identity

- A peer is a WebCrypto signing keypair (Ed25519, ECDSA P-256 where Ed25519 is missing) plus an ECDH P-256 keypair for sealing. `PeerId` is the base32 public signing key.
- The keypair is made on the first visit and kept in IndexedDB; the private key is non-extractable, so not even the page can read it. Clearing site data is a new identity.
- A `PeerId` is 52 base32 characters for an Ed25519 key (32 bytes) or 104 for a raw P-256 point (65 bytes); the length says which. Core never holds keys: it signs through a `Signer` and verifies and hashes through `Crypto`, which the platform implements.
- Handles are self-chosen, not unique, at most 32 characters; petnames are what you call a contact. Pairing shows a 16-hex-character fingerprint (the first 8 bytes of the id's SHA-256, spelled in fours) to compare.
- Everyone's picture is a pixel pigeon drawn from their id ([design.md](design.md#avatars)). `hello` says which version of the drawing to use, so a newer version never changes the bird of someone who already has one.
- The sealing (ECDH) keypair is made and announced in `hello` from M7, when async invites need it; letters use it too.

## Devices

One person can use Pigeon on several devices ([0024](decisions/0024-a-signed-list-of-devices.md)). Each device has its own non-extractable key, and an identity is a device log (`devices/device-log.ts`). The first device's key starts the log and stays the identity's `PeerId`, so friends, fingerprints and avatars never change; it's on the list without an entry.

One device writes the log at a time: the first device, until a recovery key names another (below). Entries `{ seq, prev, kind, device, name?, at, sig }` are signed by the writer over the entry and the identity, each naming the signature of the one before: `add` puts a device on, named; `remove` takes one off for good within its epoch (its key can't be added again, and the writer can't be removed); `name` renames one. With one writer each epoch's entries are a single chain, and a copy further along it replaces a shorter one; a copy that disagrees on an entry both have is ignored. A log holds at most 100 entries across all its epochs. When a merge would hold more, the oldest epochs lose their last entries first, and the current epoch is never cut. No other device can change the list, so a stolen second device can do nothing to it.

A recovery key outranks every device ([0027](decisions/0027-a-recovery-key-that-outranks-devices.md), [0030](decisions/0030-the-recovery-key-beside-the-chain.md)). It's an Ed25519 key whose public half the first device commits to once, as `recovery: { key, at, sig }`, signed over `{ identity, type: "recovery-key", key, at }`. Where two copies of a log name different keys, the one you had first stays. A recovery is an item of `epochs`: `{ recover, entries }`, where `recover` is `{ epoch, writer, keep: [{ device, name }], next, at, sig }`, signed by the key current before it (`recovery.key` for epoch 1, the previous `next` after) over `{ identity, type: "recover", ... }`. Its `keep` (up to 8 devices, the writer among them) is the list from then on, and every other device is removed as of its `at`. The writer signs that epoch's entries, whose signed bytes add `epoch`. Each epoch stands on its own: its recover's signature and its own chain decide whether it holds, and the list comes from the latest epoch alone. An older epoch's entries only say who was once yours and until when, so a stolen device that writes more of them after a recovery changes nothing and can't make a log fail. Where two copies disagree on an epoch's recover, the one you hold stays if you hold a later epoch on top of it, so a used and replaced key can't start its epoch again ([0031](decisions/0031-a-replaced-recovery-key-stays-replaced.md)). At your latest epoch the lower signature wins, with everything after it. Copies that agree take the one with more epochs, and within an epoch the longer copy of the same chain wins. Committing a key also signs a `name` entry for the first device, so a Pigeon from before, which takes a log only when it has more entries and then keeps it whole, keeps the commitment too. Epoch 0's entries and their bytes are unchanged, so Pigeons from before read them and ignore `recovery` and `epochs`. A log holds up to 8 epochs.

A recovered device's log is a tail: `from` is its first epoch, `by` the key that signed its recover, and it has nothing before that. Only a Pigeon that already has your earlier epochs takes a tail, by checking it follows on from them, and the tail's own device takes a whole log only if that log's key for its first epoch is `by`. Pairing checks whoever answers against the log you already keep for them, loaded from storage if need be. That turns away a tail from anyone you don't know, an old copy that would undo a recovery you've seen, and a device (a stolen first device, say) that speaks for itself when your copy of its log says a recovery took it off the list. A friend who keeps a log with epochs sends it back to its owner in their pair room, so a recovered device gets its whole log from the first friend it pairs with again. That friend's Pigeon knows them already, so pairing again keeps the chat and petname, takes the new pair secret and moves to its rooms.

Envelopes and chat entries are signed by a device. In a pair room a friend takes envelopes from any device active on your log, as theirs is to you, and chat log authors are devices, so sequence numbers never collide; presence shows the newest sent. A removed device's entries from before its removal (by their time, which is the device's own word) still count, so a device linked later catches up with no gaps; nothing later is taken from it. A device that finds itself removed stops talking. Your own other devices in the same pair room catch the chat up with each other (`log-sync`, `log-entries`) but aren't the friend. An identity with a log sends it as `device-log` before `hello`, in every pair room and again whenever it changes; friends keep it under `devices:<id>`. `hello` carries `devices: true`, and a friend whose `hello` lacks it is on a Pigeon from before device logs: it hears only your first device, and a second device's chat says so until they update.

A copied browser profile holds the same device key as the original. Every `hello` carries `instance`, 10 random bytes in base32 made each time the Messenger starts, and a device that hears a hello signed by its own key with another `instance` knows it's open in another browser too ([0028](decisions/0028-one-tab-and-copied-profiles.md)). Only one tab per browser runs the Messenger, so a second tab is never mistaken for a copy.

Linking is in person, from the first device. It hosts `link:<code>` (a code like an invite's, for 10 minutes) and shows it as the link `#l/<code><fingerprint>` in a QR code and in letters. The new device, which must have no friends or devices of its own (a Pigeon that has them can leave first, telling its friends and forgetting everything, [0035](decisions/0035-leave-a-pigeon-to-link-it.md)), joins and sends `link-request`; the existing one answers `link-offer` with whose it is (and its device log, if it isn't the first device), and both show the same 6-digit check, from SHA-256 of `pigeon/link/v1`, the code and both device keys. Confirming on the first device signs `add` and sends `link-accept` with a bundle (refused beforehand, with nothing signed, when it wouldn't fit one envelope): the identity, the device log, the self secret, the profile, the friends (with their pair secrets), the blocked list and friends' device logs. Chats aren't in it; they catch up through pair rooms. The new device keeps it all and starts again as that identity. Profiles are copied then and not kept in step after; M8's self space syncs your own data.

Your devices meet in `self:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(self secret, "pigeon/self/v1/" + UTC date)>`, locked with HKDF of the self secret (info `pigeon/self/password`) and renamed daily like pair rooms. Only device logs travel there, so a removal reaches your other devices with no friend online. A device that finds itself removed stops talking and says so.

## Your data in a file

Export My Data writes a `.txt` whose sealed line is `pigeon-sealed:v1:<rounds>:<salt>:<iv>:<sealed>` (base64), made with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 rounds) and AES-256-GCM from a passphrase ([0029](decisions/0029-your-data-in-a-file-for-your-own-devices.md)). Inside it is the canonical JSON `{ kind: "pigeon-export", v: 1, at, self, profile, friends, blocked, friendDevices, logs, settings }`, where `logs` maps each friend to their chat log as stored. It holds no keys, device log or self secret. Import refuses a file whose `self` isn't this device's identity, merges each chat's entries with the same checks a catch-up uses, adds friends and blocks this device lacks (keeping its own where both have one), takes the profile and settings only on a device with no name yet, and starts the Messenger again.

## Rooms

All traffic goes through the `Transport` interface ([0002](decisions/0002-trystero-behind-a-transport-interface.md)).

Inside a room the transport names connections (`ConnectionId`); those are not identities. A connection becomes a friend only by sending an envelope signed by that friend's key, starting with `hello`.

- `pair:<base32 HMAC-SHA256(secret, "pigeon/pair/v2/" + UTC date)>`: where two friends meet, named from the 256-bit pair secret only they hold, so it's a new name each UTC day (`2026-11-24`) and knowing their ids says nothing ([0026](decisions/0026-pair-rooms-from-a-shared-secret.md)). Chat, buzz, typing, files, letters. It's joined with a Trystero password, base32 HKDF-SHA256 of the secret (no salt, info `pigeon/pair/password`, 32 bytes), so offers can't be read and the room can't be entered without it. In the last hour of a UTC day tomorrow's room is joined too, and in the first hour yesterday's, so clocks less than an hour apart still meet; one pair session spans the rooms (`pairing/pair-rooms.ts`).
- `pair:<hash of both ids>`, the old pair room: base32 SHA-256 of `pigeon/pair/v1`, then the two ids sorted, newline-separated. Anyone who knows both ids can compute it, so it's only used to move friends from before 0026 (see Pairing), and it stays the chat's id: pair envelopes and chat entries are signed for it in every pair room.
- `self:<daily name>`: where your own devices meet, from the self secret (see Devices).
- `link:<code>`: one per device link, used once (see Devices).
- `invite:<code>`: one per invite, used once for pairing ([0016](decisions/0016-pairing-through-one-use-invite-rooms.md), [0018](decisions/0018-invite-codes-and-short-links.md)).
- `page:<owner id>`: one per homepage. Snapshot requests and guestbook entries.
- `lobby`: opt-in public presence.

## Messages

Every message is an envelope `{ v: 1, from, room, at, body, sig }` in canonical JSON (keys sorted), at most 64 KB (files are chunked). `sig` is `from`'s base32 signature over the envelope without `sig`, so naming the room stops an envelope being replayed into another one. In pair rooms `room` is the chat's id (the old pair room's name) whichever daily room carried it, so a friend in two rooms at once sends the same envelope to both; `buzz`, `unfriend`, `pair-secret` and `pair-secret-ok` are taken once. Unsigned, oversized, malformed, wrong-room or blocked-sender messages are dropped, as is anything in a pair room from a key other than the friend's.

## Pairing

An invite is an 8-character base32 code (40 bits, shown as `K7RT-VP2B`) that pairs once and is hosted for 30 minutes, also across reloads ([0018](decisions/0018-invite-codes-and-short-links.md)). It is shared typed, or as the link `https://pigeon.tomszir.com/#i/<code><fingerprint>`, which adds the inviter's fingerprint in base32 (23 characters after `#`); the QR code is the link. Typing accepts either case, with or without the dash, and reads 0, 1 and 8 as o, l and b. Opening a link takes it out of the address bar.

The inviter waits in `invite:<code>`. The invitee joins and sends `pair-request` (code, their name); the inviter answers `pair-offer` (code, its name). From a device that isn't its identity's first, either also carries `identity` and its device log, so the friend is saved as the identity and the fingerprint shown (and checked against a link's) is the identity's. The invitee is shown the name and fingerprint: from a link, an offer whose fingerprint doesn't match the link's is refused; from a typed code, they're warned to compare it with their friend's screen. Only when they choose Add does `pair-confirm` go back; the inviter adds them, makes a 32-byte pair secret, answers `pair-accept` with it (52 base32 characters), and stops hosting the code. Both then save each other as friends, with the secret, and meet in their pair room. Both meet only in the secret's rooms from the start and never join the old room, whose offers anyone who knows both ids could read. An invitee whose Pigeon was from before 0026 ignores the secret, and the two have to pair again.

Friends from before 0026 have no secret and meet in the old room. The first time both are there, the one with the lower id makes a secret (or uses the one it has) and sends `pair-secret`; the other keeps it, answers `pair-secret-ok`, and both join the secret's rooms and leave the old one for good. A friend who is in a secret's room has proved they hold it, which also counts as moving. Until a friend comes back, you keep waiting for them in the old room. Removing a friend keeps the secret with the farewell, so `unfriend` finds them wherever they are.

All rooms, pair and invite alike, can go through the TURN relay only (File, Always Go Through the Relay in the Messenger, or Connection in Settings; `iceTransportPolicy: "relay"`), so friends never see your IP address; it's slower and needs the relay to be reachable ([0022](decisions/0022-turn-from-a-worker-route.md)). It fails closed: with no relay to be had, Pigeon doesn't fall back to connecting directly, and a balloon (at most once a minute) says the relay can't be reached and where to turn the setting off.

While it waits, the invitee's Add a Friend shows each real step as it happens (`openInvite`'s `onStep`): reaching the relays (the room is joined), finding whoever made the code (a peer connected and was sent `pair-request`), and asking who they are (until `pair-offer`), then, after Add, waiting for `pair-accept`. A paper plane glides one way along a dotted path from you to your friend, its tip on the dots, fading in and out; the bar keeps moving, a countdown shows when it gives up (45 seconds for an answer, 20 for the accept), and a hint says what to check when a step stalls (`apps/messenger/connecting/`). Once paired, both sides go straight to the conversation, which opens with "Toms and Mika became friends on November 24 at 6:38 PM.", drawn from the friend's `addedAt`, so it stays on every visit.

## Removing and blocking

Removing a friend deletes them and your chat from this browser and sends `unfriend` in your pair room. If they're offline, the id (and the pair secret) waits in `farewells` and a session of its own sends it the next time they're there. Their Pigeon keeps the chat read-only, marks them "removed you", and stops its session; removing them too deletes it. Pairing again makes you friends once more. Blocking also keeps their id in `blocked`: your invites leave their `pair-request` unanswered, and an invite of theirs you open is refused before anything is agreed. Petnames and mutes are yours alone and never leave the browser.

## Logs

Chats and guestbooks are signed append-only logs: each author appends entries with a sequence number (1, 2, 3...) and a Lamport timestamp; each entry is signed together with the log's id, which for a chat is the old pair room's name. On `hello` peers exchange `log-sync` summaries (per author, the highest sequence held with no gaps) and answer with `log-entries` batches of what the other lacks. Merging is a sorted union by (Lamport, author, sequence); an entry that doesn't verify, or isn't by one of the pair, is dropped, and the first entry seen for a sequence wins. A message is at most 4000 characters. A message written while the friend is offline waits in your log and reaches them the next time you're both online; carrying it while you're apart is what letters (M4) add. Receipts and deletions are entries too.

## Letters

Sealed to the recipient (ephemeral ECDH + AES-GCM), signed by the sender, expiring after 30 days. Carriers (online mutual friends and the Lighthouse) store them opaque, deliver on reconnect, and drop them on a signed receipt. Limits: 50 letters or 20 MB per friend.

## pigeon:// addresses

`pigeon://<name>/<path>`. The name resolves petname → handle seen signed by the Lighthouse directory → raw `PeerId`; ambiguity shows a picker. Explorer joins `page:<owner>`, accepts the highest-versioned snapshot with a valid owner signature, sanitizes it with [PigeonHTML](pigeonhtml.md), and renders it. No answer: the "page not hosted" screen.
